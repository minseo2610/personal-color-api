Analyze the attached photograph and estimate the person’s best-fit
four-season personal color category.

For an assessable photograph, select exactly ONE primary label:
- spring-warm
- summer-cool
- autumn-warm
- winter-cool

INPUT CONDITIONS
There is one photograph, reportedly taken under ordinary, non-intense
white indoor lighting. Work with this photograph alone, without asking
questions or requiring additional photographs.

Treat “white lighting” as capture context, not proof of neutral color
rendering or correct camera white balance. Your result is a provisional
styling estimate from the image.

ASSESSMENT

1. Check image usability.
Assess facial visibility, exposure, blur, shadows, highlights, apparent
color casts, mixed lighting, and possible reflected color from nearby
clothing or walls.

Mention makeup, filters, hair dye, or colored contact lenses only as
visible or possible confounds. If their presence is unknown, keep it
unknown; absence of obvious signs does not establish their absence.

Separate image usability from classification confidence: a sharp,
evenly exposed photograph can still leave the season uncertain.

2. Describe the visible evidence before selecting a season.
Examine several reasonably exposed skin regions, such as the forehead,
cheeks, jaw, and visible neck. Discount localized redness, suspected
cosmetic color, shine, and deep shadows.

Assess these dimensions, allowing “uncertain” wherever necessary:
- Apparent temperature: warm-leaning, cool-leaning, or indeterminate.
- Value: the visible light-to-deep range.
- Contrast: relative lightness differences between skin, visible hair,
  eyebrows, and eyes.
- Apparent color clarity: clearer/brighter versus softer/more muted.

Distinguish what the photograph visibly shows from what you infer about
undertone or suitable clothing colors. Low saturation in skin pixels
does not establish suitability for muted palettes. Image sharpness
does not establish suitability for bright palettes.

3. Compare all four seasonal profiles.
Use these as approximate styling profiles:
- spring-warm: warm and relatively clear/bright.
- summer-cool: cool and relatively soft/muted.
- autumn-warm: warm and relatively soft/muted.
- winter-cool: cool and relatively clear/bright.

Use depth and contrast as supporting clues, rather than rigid rules.
Evaluate the whole pattern and permit insufficient evidence for any
dimension. Do not invent supporting or contradictory observations to
complete the comparison.

GUARDRAILS
- Do not assign a season from skin depth, ethnicity, hair color, or eye
  color alone. Any skin depth can occur in any seasonal family.
- Yellow-looking skin does not automatically establish warmth;
  pinkness or redness does not automatically establish coolness.
- Olive appearance is not itself a warm/cool classification.
- Dark hair or dark eyes do not automatically mean winter or autumn.
- Do not assume visible hair color is natural or infer hidden features.
- Do not treat teeth, eye whites, walls, or clothing as calibrated
  neutral references.
- Do not claim to recover true skin color or correct white balance
  merely by looking at the photograph.
- Do not invent HEX/RGB/Lab measurements, accuracy percentages, or
  calibrated probabilities.
- Do not claim to have observed responses to clothing colors or drapes
  that are absent from the photograph. Generated or imagined draping
  supplies no new evidence.

DECISION
Choose the best-supported primary label and the strongest different
alternative. The alternative may belong to the opposite temperature
family if lighting makes warmth versus coolness uncertain.

If usable evidence exists but the distinction remains weak, still give
the requested primary label and identify it as a tentative forced choice.

Use qualitative confidence: LOW or MODERATE. Reserve MODERATE for
reasonably consistent evidence with limited visible confounding.
Use LOW when decisive dimensions are uncertain, contradictory, or
sensitive to a possible lighting cast. Neither rating is a measured
probability.

If the image is missing, contains no assessable face, is monochrome,
or has too little usable color information, state that classification
is unavailable instead of inventing a label.

Confidence: [low / moderate]
